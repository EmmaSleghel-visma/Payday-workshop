03

What the deck actually is

Inherited, not authored. Knowing where it came from tells you which parts to trust and which to talk over.

It is Visma's Gemini + Copilot edition of the AI in Testing workshop — 271 slides, Google-Slides-authored, grown by accretion over more than a year. Not the Claude edition. The bones are right: the lifecycle spine, the customization toolbox and the Playwright MCP block all land for this audience. What it needed was corrections, cuts, and slides that did not exist.

Two things follow from “grown by accretion”. First, there are duplicate and stale slides, and a handful that are unreadable from the back of a room — those are the 23 cuts. Second, a lot of the content lives in the speaker notes rather than on the slides, which is why the playbook exists and why reading the deck alone would leave you improvising.

05

How the day is shaped

One arc, three surfaces, three gates. If you hold on to nothing else, hold on to the arc.

The arc. The morning is shift-left in a browser — requirements and test data, no repo, no terminal. Lunch. The afternoon is hands on keys: a green suite from nothing, then the machinery that makes it repeatable, then the full pipeline with gates, then the reveal that reframes all of it. You close by checking the day against the goals they gave you at 08:30.

Three surfaces, and say so early. They are on Cursor. You are on Copilot in VS Code on the projector. Everyone uses Gemini in a browser for the morning. The repo is configured for both editors deliberately, so your screen and their screens behave the same way — make that visible rather than hiding it. It is exactly the problem they will have when the next teammate arrives with a different tool.

The five SDLC stages are the spine, and they are what Payday asked for in writing. Every stage gets a demo, and every stage has a human gate in it. The gates are the point: AI does the volume work, a person owns the judgement, and the gates are where the handover happens. They are also the first thing teams delete.

The one number to have ready

Gross 468.749 kr. nets 376.249 kr. Gross 468.750 kr. nets 347.000 kr. One krona more gross costs the employee 29.249 kr. of net pay, and every test in the room is green. That is 14:20, and the whole day points at it.

Two to five people who have never met you. “Shout it out” is a thirty-person move; at half past eight in the morning an open question to a room this size lands in silence. The playbook gives every question a written escape, and the rule is: never let two open questions die in a row. If two die, switch to writing for ten minutes and come back to speech later.

07

When it goes wrong

Eight things that plausibly break, and what you do instead of improvising.

The room goes silent

Never ask two open questions in a row that both die. If two die, switch to writing for ten minutes — put it in the channel — and come back to speech later. Every question in the playbook carries a written escape, and the strongest rung is always answer it wrong yourself: somebody will correct you, and if nobody does you have still made the point out loud.

The Playwright MCP server will not connect

Run npx playwright run-test-mcp-server --help once to warm the npx cache, then reconnect. Do this Tuesday, not Wednesday.

The hook does not block the edit

Yours is the documented path (.github/hooks/) so it should behave; Cursor's is not documented, so check a participant's machine during the first break rather than discovering it live at 13:30.

A Gem has no Share button

Its knowledge contains a file type that cannot be shared — Google Photos items, a code folder, emails. Use plain uploads or Drive files only. This is the most likely live failure of the Gemini blocks, and it is silent.

Someone finds the tax cliff early

Do not confirm it. Say “interesting, hold that thought”, write it face-down on the whiteboard, and save it. An agent may surface it during the bug-hunter run at 13:37 — same rule.

Gunnar never sends a real bug

Fall back to R4 in facilitator/requirements/README.md. It is the rounding defect written as a user would report it, and it triages the same way.

Their suite goes red during the hands-on

That is the test-healer demo arriving early. Take it. A real failure they caused themselves beats the sabotage you were going to stage at 14:30.

You are running long

Cut in this order: the RPI section (already off the path), the Exercise slides 81 and 85, 44–45, then 91. Never cut the zero-to-green block, the gates discussion on 130, the reveal, or the commitments round.
