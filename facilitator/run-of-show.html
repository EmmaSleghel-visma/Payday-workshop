The mechanism
if (taxableBase >= BAND_1_LIMIT) {
  tax = taxableBase * BAND_2_RATE - PERSONAL_ALLOWANCE;   // the WHOLE base, not the margin

Then the three points — the payload of the whole workshop:

  • Every green test in the room passed. None of them chose 468.749 and 468.750. Round numbers all behave perfectly. Coverage was never the thing protecting you.

  • The AI can find it, and could not have decided it mattered. Nothing in the code says a tax cliff is wrong. That's a rule about the world, held by people who understand payroll. It belongs in AGENTS.md — and once written down, every future agent and every future hire gets it.

apply only to income above the threshold. Generate boundary tests that would detect a
cliff, using values one krona either side of every threshold you find.

DemoSabotage and heal117

Everyone breaks the app: in index.html, rename id="name-input" to id="employee-name". Then npm test — watch it burn.

Then
Use the test-healer agent to fix the failing tests.
It runs the suite, inspects the changed DOM, regenerates locators against reality, patches, re-runs to green.

Then stop, while it's fresh, and name the trap:

A failing test means one of two things. Either the test is stale and the app changed on purpose — heal it. Or the app is broken and the test just did its job — and healing it deletes the only warning anyone was going to get.

Here, healing was correct: the id changed deliberately. But if the healer “fixes” a test around the tax cliff, your suite is now green and lying. That's why the agent classifies every failure before touching it, leaves anything it can't classify as test.fixme(), and why healed diffs go to a human, never straight to merge.

Ask them: what would it take for you to trust an automated healer on your product? The answers are their own governance model, arrived at by themselves.
